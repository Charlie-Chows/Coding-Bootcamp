About Stackly

A bootcamp built for people who want to understand, not memorise.

Stackly exists because learning to code deserves more than tutorial hopping and surface-level certificates. We teach through real projects, real feedback, and a cohort that keeps you honest.

The goal isn’t to finish a course. It’s to become someone who can build the next thing on their own.

Why We Exist

Too many learners stop just short of capable.

Most people who want to become developers don’t fail because the material is too hard. They stop because the path is scattered, feedback is missing, and nothing they build feels finished.

Stackly was designed around that gap. A fixed sequence so nobody gets lost. Real projects so progress is visible. Mentors who read your code so you stop guessing whether you’re doing it right.

  • A structured path from fundamentals to a deployed full-stack build.
  • Human feedback on the code you actually wrote, not a hypothetical.
  • Honest expectations: no placement guarantees, no invented statistics.

Learning Philosophy

Practice over theory. Clarity over cleverness.

We believe skill grows through repeated, focused effort on real problems — not from watching someone else solve them.

Build to understand

Concepts are introduced because a project needs them, not because a syllabus says so.

Read before writing

Debugging, code review, and reading unfamiliar code are core skills, not side quests.

Refactor to grow

You revisit your own work as your skill increases. Improvement becomes visible.

Explain to learn

Being able to describe your decisions out loud is the final step of understanding them.

Project-First Education

You learn the thing by making the thing.

Every Stackly module is anchored to a build with clear acceptance criteria. You know what done looks like before you start, and you know exactly what to demonstrate when you finish.

  • Briefs written like real tickets, not classroom exercises.
  • Architecture decisions discussed before the first commit.
  • Reviews happen on a pull request, with comments tied to lines of code.

Mentors & Teaching Approach

Teachers who ask why before they say what.

Stackly mentors are practising engineers and educators who’ve spent real time in codebases that other people depend on. Their job isn’t to hand you answers — it’s to make your thinking sharper.

  • They start by asking what you intended, then show where the code disagrees.
  • They offer alternatives with tradeoffs, not just corrections.
  • They pair with you when a concept refuses to click.
  • They treat questions as contribution, never as interruption.

We describe the approach rather than publishing individual mentor profiles. Real biographies and credentials would be added with permission before launch.

Learning Culture & Community

A cohort that keeps momentum visible.

Learning to code alone is possible. Learning to code alongside people who are stuck on similar problems is faster, kinder, and far more likely to last.

Peer review rounds

Before a mentor sees your work, a peer does. Both reviews make you sharper.

Shared milestones

Weekly checkpoints keep progress visible and make setbacks easier to name.

Supportive by design

Asking questions is treated as contribution. Nobody is expected to know it already.

Feedback & Code Review

Feedback that points at the code, not the person.

The most useful feedback is specific, timely, and attached to the actual lines you wrote. At Stackly, that’s the default — not a premium add-on.

  • Reviews happen on pull requests, so context is preserved.
  • Comments explain the reasoning, not just the correction.
  • Strengths are named as clearly as weaknesses.
  • You can respond, push follow-up commits, and close the loop.

Accessibility & Learner Support

Built to be learnable by more people, not fewer.

Accessibility isn’t a checklist at the end of a project. It shapes how we teach, how we review, and what we expect from every build you submit.

Keyboard-first habits

Every interface you build is tested with the keyboard before it’s considered done.

Readable contrast

Colour choices are checked for legibility, not just visual appeal.

Multiple formats

Explanations come as written notes, live sessions, and short recorded walkthroughs.

Flexible pacing

Where a module can be revisited without penalty, it is. Learning isn’t linear.

Support conversations

If something isn’t working, there’s a real person to talk to about it.

Safe to be stuck

Being stuck is treated as a normal part of engineering, not a failure of effort.

Our accessibility commitments

Semantic markup

Structure that assistive technology and future you can both read.

Tests beside the feature

Not bolted on later. Written while the intent is fresh.

Readable history

Documentation as a habit

Review-ready PRs

Practical Engineering Standards

The habits that outlast any framework.

Frameworks change. The disciplines that make a codebase maintainable don’t. Stackly teaches those deliberately, because they’re what hiring teams actually test for once the interview starts.

You’ll be asked to write code that another learner can pick up, run, and reason about. That constraint quietly improves everything you build.

What to Expect

An honest picture of the learner experience.

Stackly is demanding, structured, and designed to be finished. Here’s what the shape of the experience actually looks like.

  1. Onboarding & setup

    Tool setup, environment checks, and a short orientation so week one starts with code, not configuration.

  2. Foundations with feedback

    Small builds every week, each one reviewed. The point is repetition with correction, not speed.

  3. Project lab

    Larger, open-ended builds where the decisions are yours and the review is deeper.

  4. Full-stack capstone

    A complete product, deployed and documented, presented to your cohort and mentor.

  5. Career preparation

    Portfolio review, technical conversation practice, and honest feedback on where you are.

Stackly does not promise job placement, salaries, or certification outcomes. What we commit to is a structured curriculum, real feedback, and a finished portfolio you can stand behind.

Where We’re Going

A commitment to keep learning, in public.

Stackly is young, and we’d rather be honest about that than pretend otherwise. What we can promise is a bias toward improvement: listening to learners, adjusting the curriculum, and staying close to what real engineering teams actually need.

  • Learner feedback shapes the next cohort’s modules.
  • Curriculum is revised with each run, not locked in place.
  • More public writing on teaching, accessibility, and workflow.
  • Deeper peer-review structures as cohorts grow.
