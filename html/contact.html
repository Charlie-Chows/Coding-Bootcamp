What People Ask About

The questions we get most often.

If your question is on this list, you’re in good company. If it isn’t, that’s also fine — ask anyway.

  • Which program should I start with?

    Depends on what you already know. We’ll ask a few questions and point you at the right entry point.

  • Can I do this alongside a job?

    Programs are paced for learners who can commit focused hours each week rather than full-time immersion.

  • What support is available?

    Mentor review, peer review, open support conversations, and accessibility accommodations where needed.

  • What’s not offered?

    No placement guarantees, no accredited certification, no promises about salaries. We’d rather be clear about that up front.

Response Process

How a message becomes an answer.

No call centre, no ticket queue. Just a short, human process we’ve kept deliberately simple.

  1. Message arrives

    Whether by email, phone note, or form — everything lands in one inbox.

  2. We read it carefully

    A mentor or team member reads the whole message before replying.

  3. We reply honestly

    Sometimes with questions, sometimes with a recommendation, sometimes with “not yet.”

  4. You take it from there

    Apply, ask more, or decide later. No automated follow-up sequences.

Support Expectations

What support honestly looks like here.

Support at Stackly isn’t a slogan. It’s a set of specific, repeated behaviours we hold ourselves to when learners get stuck.

  • Timely replies

    Messages reach a person, not a void. Replies usually come within 1–2 working days.

  • Honest answers

    If something isn’t a good fit, we say so. We’d rather lose an applicant than mislead one.

  • Accessibility accommodation

    If you need format adjustments or pacing flexibility, ask. It’s a normal conversation here.

  • No pressure follow-up

    We won’t chase you with automated sequences. The next step is always yours.

“Supportive” isn’t a marketing word here — it’s the standard every reply is held to.

Preparing for the First Conversation

A few things worth having ready.

You don’t need to prepare a pitch. But a little thinking ahead makes the reply we send back much more useful.

Where you are now

Have you coded before? Followed tutorials? Built anything? A sentence or two is plenty.

What you want to build

A website, an app, a tool for your own work — the more specific, the better.

Time you can commit

An honest number of focused hours each week — not an aspirational one.

Support you might need

Accessibility accommodations, learning preferences, or format adjustments.

If you don’t have all of this yet, that’s fine. We’d rather help you figure it out than have you wait until everything’s tidy.
